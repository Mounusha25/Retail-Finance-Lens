# Retail Finance Lens — Performance & Working Capital Analytics

## Analyst report — Walmart, Target, and Costco

**Prepared:** 2026-10-14  
**Decision question:** Which retailer is growing profitably, managing inventory efficiently, and converting profit into cash?  
**Scope:** Latest five completed annual periods and twelve standalone quarters per company, based on latest-known SEC filings.

---

## Executive conclusion

**Costco currently presents the strongest combination of growth and operating efficiency.** Its latest net sales grew 10.1%, inventory turned 14.1×, and the cash conversion cycle was only 1.0 day. Walmart remains the peer group's largest absolute cash generator, producing $14.9B of free cash flow. Target retained the highest operating margin at 4.9%, but its -2.0% sales decline and slower inventory turns make the quality of that margin the most important issue to investigate.

This is not a single-winner story. Costco leads on momentum and working-capital speed; Walmart leads on cash scale; Target shows that higher margin does not automatically imply stronger growth or inventory productivity.

## Findings and financial implications

### 1. Costco combines the fastest growth with the strongest inventory productivity

Costco's latest net sales increased 10.1% to $297.2B. Gross margin was 11.1% and operating margin was 3.9%, while inventory turned 14.1×—well ahead of Walmart and Target. Its 1.0-day cash conversion cycle indicates that very little cash remained tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983226000093/cost-20260830.htm)

**Implication.** The low-margin model is economically attractive because sales velocity and supplier financing reduce the working capital required to support growth. Operating cash flow of $15.8B covered capex of $6.4B and left $9.4B of free cash flow. The main analytical question is therefore not whether Costco can move inventory, but whether membership economics and warehouse expansion can sustain this pace without weakening returns on incremental capital.

### 2. Walmart is the cash-flow leader, but its liquidity profile deserves context

Walmart generated $41.6B of operating cash flow and $14.9B of free cash flow—the highest absolute amount in the group. OCF/net income of 1.9× supports the quality of reported earnings. Net sales grew 4.7%, while inventory turnover reached 9.3× and the cash conversion cycle was 3.3 days. [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm)

**Implication.** Walmart's scale produces exceptional cash capacity even after $26.6B of capital investment. Its current ratio of 0.8× is below 1.0, but that should not be read in isolation as distress: high sales frequency and short operating cycles allow large retailers to operate with structurally low current ratios. The follow-up question is whether capex is translating into durable productivity and digital-margin improvement rather than merely maintaining scale.

### 3. Target protects margin, but weaker sales and slower turns create the clearest execution risk

Target's latest net sales declined 2.0%, even though its operating margin of 4.9% was the highest of the three companies. Inventory turned only 6.0×, versus 9.3× at Walmart and 14.1× at Costco. Target still produced $2.8B of free cash flow and converted earnings into operating cash at 1.8×. [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27419/000002741926000016/tgt-20260131.htm)

**Implication.** Margin resilience is positive, but declining sales combined with lower inventory productivity can signal mix pressure, markdown exposure, weaker traffic, or excess capacity. The next investigation should separate comparable sales, traffic, average ticket, shrink, markdowns, advertising revenue, and category mix. Target's reported cash cycle is short, but it relies on annual receivable data; quarterly cash-cycle analysis remains unavailable rather than being estimated.

### 4. Reported profit is supported by cash across all three peers

OCF/net income was 1.7× for Costco, 1.8× for Target, and 1.9× for Walmart. Each ratio was above 1.0, and all three companies reported positive free cash flow. That reduces concern that current profit is primarily accrual-driven.

**Implication.** One year of strong conversion is supportive, not conclusive. Cash conversion can be temporarily lifted by inventory reductions, supplier timing, or tax and compensation accruals. The quarterly cash-flow bridge should therefore be monitored alongside the five-year trend before concluding that the latest conversion rate is sustainable.

## Recommended areas for investigation

1. **Costco:** decompose growth into comparable sales, new warehouses, membership-fee growth, and foreign exchange; compare incremental operating profit with incremental capex.
2. **Walmart:** evaluate returns from store, supply-chain, automation, and e-commerce investment; normalize working-capital timing before extrapolating the latest cash conversion ratio.
3. **Target:** reconcile sales pressure with margin resilience through traffic, ticket, category mix, markdowns, shrink, and advertising/credit-card revenue; monitor inventory growth against sales each quarter.
4. **Across peers:** compare supplier-term changes and lease-adjusted obligations, then extend the analysis to segment-level and comparable-sales disclosures before making capital-allocation conclusions.

## Methodology, controls, and limitations

The latest validation run recorded **113 passed checks, 0 warnings, and 0 errors**. Revenue components reconcile for all displayed annual periods and quarters. Raw SEC responses, accession numbers, filing dates, source concepts, units, dimensions, and derived-fact status are retained.

- Net sales—not consolidated revenue—drives growth and gross margin. Membership and other revenue remains separate.
- Average beginning and ending balances drive inventory, receivable, and payable efficiency. Actual fiscal-day counts drive days metrics.
- Standalone quarterly flows are derived from cumulative disclosures when necessary; Q4 equals the annual value less Q3 YTD. Instant balances are never differenced.
- Fiscal years are aligned by calendar year of period end but are not identical economic windows. Two displayed annual periods are 53-week years and are flagged rather than normalized away.
- Missing facts remain null. Total-liability mapping gaps produce the 12 current warnings; no missing amount is silently treated as zero.
- Target has 12 missing quarterly cash-cycle observations because a comparable quarterly receivables series is not disclosed under the configured concepts.
- The analysis is consolidated-company, nominal USD, and U.S. GAAP. It excludes segments, constant-currency adjustments, leases beyond reported debt fields, valuation, and stock-price performance.
- Latest-known facts power the dashboard. Historical versions remain stored, but this report is not an as-of-date investment backtest.
